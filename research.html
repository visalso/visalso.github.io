Current work · Caltech

Scalable neutral-atom quantum computing

As an IQIM Postdoctoral Scholar in the Endres Lab at Caltech, I work on next-generation, large-scale quantum computers based on individually controlled neutral-atom arrays.

My broader goal is to develop hardware-efficient tools and protocols from the ground up, adding the control needed to explore regimes that are difficult to access with classical methods.

Trapped-ion quantum simulation

Programmable open quantum systems

How do coherent interactions and the surrounding environment shape quantum dynamics? My doctoral research in Guido Pagano’s Quantum Simulation Lab at Rice University explored this question using trapped ions and chemically motivated models.

By engineering both coherent and dissipative interactions, we investigated electron transfer, charge and exciton transfer, and tunable thermal reservoirs at the interface of quantum information, AMO physics, condensed-matter physics, and chemical physics.

Thermal reservoirs with tunable temperature

Realizing tunable thermal reservoirs in a trapped-ion spin-boson simulator extends the ways we can study open-system dynamics.

Experimental tools

Quantum hardware and control

Quantum simulation depends on the devices and controls that make precise experiments possible. My work includes a collaboration on monolithic segmented three-dimensional ion traps for quantum technology applications.

I am interested in developing experimental tools that connect practical hardware capabilities with new scientific questions.
